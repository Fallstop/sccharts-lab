A railway junction is not a station: trains pass over it without stopping, and which way they go
depends on how the points are set. A **connector state** is a junction for transitions. Control
arrives at it and leaves it again in the same tick, along the first of its outgoing transitions
whose trigger holds.

Connectors add no new behaviour to the language. They let you write a decision once, at the point
where several paths meet, instead of copying it onto every path.

## A junction in a vending machine

A drink costs 30 cents, and the machine takes 10c and 20c coins. Both coin transitions add to
`credit` and lead to the connector `Check`, which decides whether to vend:

<Sim example="advanced/connectors/vending" caption="Each coin passes through `Check` in the tick it is inserted. In tick 4 of the tour, `credit` reaches 30 and is spent in the same tick." />

In tick 4 the 10c coin brings `credit` to 30. The transition into `Check` and the transition out of
it run in the same tick, one after the other: `credit += 10`, then the test `credit >= 30`, which sees
the new value, then `credit -= 30; vend`. Without a coin, the machine stays in `Waiting`. It never
rests in `Check`.

The ticks of the tour in which a coin arrives:

| Tick | Coin | `credit` after | `vend` |
| ---: | --- | --- | --- |
| 2 | 20c | 20 | absent |
| 4 | 10c | 0 | present |
| 6 | 20c | 20 | absent |
| 8 | 20c and 10c | 10 | present |

Tick 8 shows the priorities at work: both coins arrive in one tick, and `Waiting`'s first transition
(the 20c one) wins: 20 + 20 = 40 vends, with 10 left over. The 10c coin is ignored: the machine keeps
it, and the customer has lost 10 cents. A real machine would handle the two coins together, with a first
transition `if coin10 && coin20 do credit += 30 go to Check`.

## The rule

A connector is declared with the keyword `connector`: `connector state Check`. The diagram draws it as
a small filled dot, without its name. Then:

- **Its outgoing transitions are always immediate**, whether or not they say `immediate`. Control that
  reaches a connector leaves it in the same tick, as in a chain of
  [immediate transitions](/learn/immediate/). The diagram draws them dashed, like every immediate
  transition.
- **They are tried in priority order**, like the outgoing transitions of any state: the first one whose
  trigger holds is taken. Give the last one no trigger, so that one of them always fires.
- **They see what the incoming transition did.** Effects run in sequence along the whole path, so the
  test `credit >= 30` reads the `credit` that the coin transition has just updated.

A connector may also have entry actions, which run as control passes through, and it may be the
initial state of a region (see [below](#starting-with-a-decision)).

KIELER implements a connector as an ordinary state whose outgoing transitions are made immediate. The
keyword is a convenience and a piece of notation: it tells the reader, and the compiler's checks, that
this state is only a branching point.

## Why connectors help

Here is the same machine without a connector. Each coin now needs two transitions of its own, one that
vends and one that does not, and each vending guard has to work out the new credit in advance:

<Compare
  a="advanced/connectors/vending"
  b="advanced/connectors/vending-without"
  scenario="advanced/connectors/vending-compare"
  caption="The same coins go into both machines, and they always agree. Switch to Code to compare them: the price, 30, appears twice in `Vending` and four times in `VendingWithout`."
/>

Both charts behave the same in every tick. But in `VendingWithout` the price appears in four places,
and a 50c coin would need two more transitions, one of them with yet another copy of the test. With
the connector, a new coin is one new transition into `Check`, and the test is written once. The bigger the decision,
the more this matters: with *n* ways in and *m* ways out, a connector needs *n* + *m* transitions
instead of *n* × *m*.

Connectors are also handy right after a superstate finishes: `join to Decide` followed by a connector
that looks at what the superstate computed and picks the next state.

## Starting with a decision

A connector can be a region's initial state. Then the region makes its first decision in the tick it
starts. This fan starts in `High` or `Low`, depending on `boost` in tick 1:

<Sim example="advanced/connectors/initial-connector" caption="Tick 1 passes through `Start` and enters `High` because `boost` is true. Set `boost` to false and reset to start in `Low`." />

Because a connector is left in the tick it is entered, the region is never "in" `Start` at the end of a
tick, and nothing leads back to it, so `boost` matters only in tick 1.

## What KIELER warns about

Two habits keep a connector honest, and KIELER warns when you break them.

**Write `immediate` on its outgoing transitions.** They are immediate anyway, but without the keyword
the chart reads as if they were delayed. KIELER warns, once for each such transition,

```text wrap
Outgoing transitions of connector states should be marked as immediate.
```

and treats them as immediate. Writing `if credit >= 30 do ...` instead of
`immediate if credit >= 30 do ...` in the vending machine changes nothing in its behaviour.

**Give it an outgoing transition without a trigger.** Otherwise there may be ticks in which no way out
is open, and then control simply stays in the connector, as in a normal state:

<Sim example="advanced/connectors/resting" caption="In tick 2 `enough` is false, so control stops in `Check` and waits there until tick 4." />

KIELER warns

```text wrap
Connector states should have an outgoing transition without trigger.
```

The chart still works: control stays in `Check`, and its transitions are checked again in every tick.
But a connector that can hold control is a state in disguise. If waiting is what you mean, use a normal
state.

## What KIELER rejects

**A connector with no way out.** A connector must have at least one outgoing transition. Without one,
KIELER rejects the chart:

```text wrap
Connector states must have an outgoing transition.
```


<Chart example="advanced/connectors/dead-end" caption="`Check` has no outgoing transition: KIELER rejects the chart." />

**Connectors in a cycle.** Connectors that lead back to each other, directly or through other immediate
transitions, form a cycle of immediate transitions. Below, `Add` and `Check` send control round and
round, and the guard `coins < 3` would end the cycle after three rounds. KIELER rejects the chart all
the same: it rejects every cycle of immediate transitions, whatever its guards (see
[Delayed and immediate transitions](/learn/immediate/#immediate-loops-are-rejected)).

<Chart example="advanced/connectors/loop" caption="`Add` and `Check` form a cycle of immediate transitions: KIELER rejects the chart, although the guard would end the cycle." />

The editor warns, once for each connector,

```text wrap
There is an immediate loop. The model is not SASC.
Involved states: [Check, Add]
```

and compiling fails with `The SCG is NOT asc-schedulable!`. To repeat something, let a
tick pass: go through a normal state with a delayed transition.

## Check your understanding

<Quiz
  question="Which of these makes KIELER reject a chart, rather than just warn?"
  options={[
    'A connector whose outgoing transitions are not marked `immediate`',
    'A connector without an untriggered outgoing transition',
    'A connector with no outgoing transitions at all',
    'A connector as the initial state of a region',
  ]}
  answer={2}
>
A connector with no way out is an error: `Connector states must have an outgoing transition.` The first
two are only warnings (the transitions are immediate anyway; without a default, control may rest in the
connector), and an initial connector is fine.
</Quiz>

<Quiz
  question="A delayed transition `if coin do credit += 10 go to Check` leads into a connector whose first transition is `immediate if credit >= 30 do vend go to Waiting`. `credit` was 20 and `coin` is true in this tick. What happens?"
  options={[
    '`credit` becomes 30, and `vend` is emitted in the same tick',
    '`credit` becomes 30, and `vend` is emitted in the next tick, when the connector is checked',
    "`vend` is not emitted: the connector's test reads `credit` from the start of the tick",
  ]}
  answer={0}
>
Control passes through a connector within the tick, and the effects along the path run in order, so the
test sees `credit` = 30 and the connector's first transition fires at once.
</Quiz>

<KeyIdeas>
- A connector (`connector state C`, drawn as a dot) is a junction: control passes through it within the
  tick.
- Its outgoing transitions are always immediate and are tried in priority order; they see the effects of
  the transition that led in.
- Use a connector to write a decision once where several paths meet, or as an initial state that picks
  where a region starts.
- Mark its transitions `immediate` and give it an untriggered last transition, or KIELER warns (and
  control may stop in it).
- A connector without outgoing transitions, or a cycle of connectors, is rejected.
</KeyIdeas>
