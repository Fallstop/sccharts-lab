Your phone is playing the second track of an album when a call comes in. After the call you expect
the music to carry on with track 2, not to start the album again. A transition into a
[superstate](/learn/hierarchy/) normally does start it again, from its initial states. A **history
transition** brings it back to where it was when it was left.

The second half of this chapter is about a different modifier for transitions into a state,
`deferred`, which makes the arrival quiet: the target state does nothing in the tick it is entered.

## Coming back where you left off

Both players below leave `Playing` for `OnCall` when `call` is pressed, and go back when `hangUp` is
pressed. The only difference is the last line: `PlayerRestart` goes back with `go to Playing`,
`PlayerHistory` with `go to Playing history`.

## The rule

`go to S history` enters `S` in the states that were active in each of its regions when `S` was last
left, at every level of nesting. The first time `S` is entered this way, there is nothing to
remember, and it starts from its initial states as usual.

Three details matter in practice:

- **The resumed states are entered again.** Their entry actions run, and their immediate transitions
  are checked, just as for any other entry. In tick 5, `Track2`'s entry action sets `track` back to 2.
- **History remembers the state that was entered last.** In tick 7, `next` and `call` arrive together.
  `go to OnCall` is a weak abort, so the inside of `Playing` first takes its last step, from `Track2`
  to `Track3` (see [Aborts](/learn/preemption/)). That step counts: in tick 8, history resumes
  `Track3`. The one exception is a state entered with `deferred`, which history does not record at
  all ([below](#deferred-entries-are-not-remembered)).
- **History belongs to the transition, not to the state.** Every other transition into `S` still
  starts it from its initial states. You can say so explicitly with `go to S reset`, which is the
  same as writing nothing.

<Aside title="The _H warnings">
A chart with history compiles with warnings such as

```text wrap
ValuedObject with name _HPlaying is contained in the model but not in VariableStore.
```

KIELER keeps each remembered state in a hidden variable `_H<state>`, and the warning is about that variable: one for
the target of each history transition, and with deep history one more for every superstate inside it
whose position is remembered too. The warnings are harmless.
</Aside>

## History restores states, not data

History brings back where you were, but not what your variables held. Here `Playing` counts the
seconds of the current track in a local variable, `seconds`, and copies it to the output `shown`. A
call comes in during tick 4, and the player is resumed with history in tick 5. What does `shown` say
in tick 6? Predict, check, then open the explanation.

<Predict
  example="advanced/history/locals-reset"
  ask={[{ tick: 6, value: 'shown' }]}
  caption="`seconds` is declared inside `Playing`. Does history bring back its value?"
/>

<details>
<summary>Explanation</summary>

`shown` is 1. Resuming with history is still an *entry* of `Playing`, and a state's local variables
are initialised every time the state is entered (see [Superstates](/learn/hierarchy/)). So `seconds`
starts again from 0 while `Track` is resumed. The same goes for the locals of the resumed inner
states, and for their entry actions, which run again.

</details>

If a value must survive the visit, declare it outside the superstate, or mark it **`static`**:

```sctx
initial state Playing {
  static int seconds = 0
```

A `static` local is initialised once, when the chart starts, and keeps its value when its state is left
and entered again, like a `static` local variable in C. With that one word, the player above carries on counting after the
call: `shown` is 4 in tick 6.

## Shallow and deep history

`history` on its own is *deep*: it restores every level of nesting. `shallow history` restores only the
states directly inside the target; any superstate among them starts from its own initial states.

The menu below has a `Settings` superstate with two pages, `Display` and `Sound`, each a superstate
with two screens. The screen shown is a number in `screen`: 11 and 12 for the two display screens, 21
and 22 for the two sound screens, 0 for `Home`.

The value of `screen` after each tick, from tick 2:

| Tick | Inputs | Deep | Shallow |
| ---: | --- | --- | --- |
| 2 | `menu` | 11 | 11 |

| 8 | `home` | 0 | 0 |
| 9 | `menu` | 22 | 21 |

Both kinds remember which page was open. Only deep history also remembers the screen within the page.

## A history transition that is silently ignored

Now put the menu inside a device that can be switched off. `Home` and `Settings` move into a superstate
`On`, next to an `Off` state:

<Compare
  a="advanced/history/nested-ignored"
  b="advanced/history/nested-fixed"
  scenario="advanced/history/nested-compare"
  caption="The tour opens `Contrast` in tick 4, goes home and opens the menu again in tick 6: `MenuNested` forgets, `MenuNestedFixed` remembers."
/>

In `MenuNested` the history transition `if menu go to Settings history` has no effect at all: in tick 6
`Settings` starts again in `Brightness`. KIELER gives no error and no warning. (The note under the
comparison is this guide's: it marks [KIELER quirks](/reference/about/#notices-on-examples) that KIELER
itself does not mention.)

The reason is a limitation of KIELER's compiler. It only processes history transitions into states of
the chart's **top-level regions**, and, inside them, history transitions into states nested in a state
that is **itself the target of a history transition**. Any other history transition is quietly
treated as a plain `go to`. `Settings` is nested inside `On`, and `On` is only ever entered with a plain
`go to On`, so the history on `Settings` is dropped.

<Aside type="danger" title="History inside a nested superstate">
A `history` or `shallow history` transition into a state that is not in a top-level region works only
if every superstate around that state is itself the target of some history transition. Otherwise it
silently restarts the state. Check the behaviour in the simulator whenever you nest a history target.
</Aside>

`MenuNestedFixed` makes `On` a history target: `Off` goes back with `go to On shallow history`. Now the
history inside `On` works (tick 6 shows `Contrast` again). But the fix also changes what switching on
does: in tick 8 the device comes back on the `Settings` page instead of `Home`. When that is not what you
want, restructure the chart so that the state you return to with history sits in a top-level region.

## Deferred transitions

A transition into a state can also be marked `deferred`: `go to S deferred`. A **deferred** entry is a
quiet one. In the tick it happens, `S` does none of its immediate behaviour:

- its entry actions do not run, and they never will for this visit;
- its `immediate during` actions and its immediate outgoing transitions wait until the next tick.

From the next tick on, `S` behaves as if it had been entered normally one tick earlier: its delayed
transitions and `during` actions can already run, and so can the immediate behaviour that waited.

In this chart, entering `Hall` rings a chime (an entry action) and walks straight on into `Room` (an
immediate transition). The transition into `Hall` is deferred:

<Sim example="advanced/history/arrive-deferred" caption="Tick 2 arrives in `Hall` quietly; the immediate transition fires in tick 3; the chime never rings." />

`chimes` and `steps` after each tick, with a normal `go to Hall` and with `go to Hall deferred`:

| Tick | `walk` | Normal | Deferred |
| ---: | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | false | 0, 0 | 0, 0 |
| 2 | true | 1, 1 | 0, 0 |
| 3 | false | 1, 1 | 0, 1 |

In the diagram, a deferred transition has a small red circle next to its arrowhead.

### What deferred is for

`deferred` is for coming back to a state whose immediate transitions should not fire again in the very
tick you arrive, because the inputs of that tick still describe the job that has just finished. This
coffee machine starts filling as soon as a cup stands under the spout, `immediate if cup do fills++ go
to Filling`, and goes back to `Ready` when the cup is `full`. The inputs of a tick are read at its
start, so in the tick of `full` the full cup is still there:

<Compare
  a="advanced/history/filler-normal"
  b="advanced/history/filler-deferred"
  scenario="advanced/history/filler-compare"
  caption="Each full cup is still under the spout in the tick it is full (ticks 3 and 6): `FillerNormal` starts filling it again at once, `FillerDeferred` waits a tick and finds it gone."
/>

With a plain `go to Ready`, `Ready`'s immediate transition reads `cup` in the same tick and starts on the
full cup again: two cups, three fills. With `go to Ready deferred`, `Ready` arrives quietly and first
looks at `cup` in the next tick, when the cup has been taken. The initial entry in tick 1 is never
deferred: both machines start on the first cup at once.

`deferred` buys exactly one tick. If a cup may stay under the spout for longer, wait until it has gone
instead, with a state between `Filling` and `Ready` that is left `if !cup`.

### Deferred entry into a superstate

For a superstate, `deferred` always skips the superstate's *own* immediate behaviour: its entry
actions, its `immediate during` actions and its immediate outgoing transitions. Whether its inside
starts a tick late as well depends, surprisingly, on the superstate's other incoming transitions. The
rule of thumb: **a deferred entry into a superstate may start its inside one tick late, so check the
timing in the simulator.**

The two charts below are identical except that `DeferredMixed` has a second, normal transition into
`Inside`, which is never even taken:

<Compare
  a="advanced/history/deferred-super-only"
  b="advanced/history/deferred-super-mixed"
  scenario="advanced/history/deferred-super-compare"
  caption="The same deferred entry in tick 2: the inside of `Inside` starts a tick late in `DeferredOnly`, on time in `DeferredMixed`."
/>

<details>
<summary>The exact rule</summary>

KIELER picks one of two implementations of `deferred` for a superstate:

- If **every** transition into the superstate is deferred, all in the same way, and the superstate cannot
  get back to itself through immediate transitions, KIELER really enters it one tick later. In the
  deferred tick nothing inside happens; in the next tick the inner initial states are entered (their
  entry actions run) and the inside starts, so its delayed transitions fire one tick later than after a
  normal entry. That is `DeferredOnly`.
- **Otherwise** the superstate is entered in the deferred tick: the entry actions of its inner initial
  states run, and the inside keeps its normal timing. That is `DeferredMixed`.

`deferred` can also be combined with history (`go to M deep deferred history`). KIELER then resumes `M`
one tick late: in the deferred tick nothing happens, and in the next tick the remembered inner state is
entered and its entry action runs, while `M`'s own entry action does not.

</details>

### Deep deferred

`go to B deep deferred` extends the quiet arrival to the inner initial states, all the way down: their
entry actions never run for this visit, and their immediate transitions wait a tick. The two charts
below differ only in the word `deep`:

<Compare
  a="advanced/history/house-deferred"
  b="advanced/history/house-deep"
  scenario="advanced/history/house-compare"
  caption="The inside of `House` starts in tick 3. `Hall`'s entry action switches on a light in `HouseDeferred` (`lights` = 1) but not in `HouseDeep`; neither chart runs `House`'s own entry action."
/>

Every transition into `House` is deferred, so in both charts the inside starts a tick late (the first
case of [the exact rule](#deferred-entry-into-a-superstate) above). `deep` decides only whether the
inner initial state `Hall` arrives quietly as well.

### Deferred entries are not remembered

KIELER keeps the history of a superstate with an entry action that it adds to every state inside: each
entry records the state. A deferred entry skips the entry actions, and that one with them. So history
does not remember a state entered with `deferred`; it resumes the state that was recorded before it:

<Sim example="advanced/history/history-deferred" caption="`Kitchen` is entered with `deferred` in tick 2. After the garden, `go to House history` in tick 5 resumes `Hall`, not `Kitchen`." />

KIELER gives no warning. If history must come back to a state, enter that state without `deferred`.

<Aside title="What the KIELER wiki says">
The KIELER wiki says that history and deferred transitions are not fully supported, may behave
unexpectedly when combined, and may not be shown correctly in the simulator. What this chapter shows is
what KIELER's simulation does. If a deferred transition seems to do something odd, step it in the
simulator and compare with the rules above.
</Aside>

## Check your understanding

<Quiz
  question="Superstate `M` was left while its inner state `J` was active. Later `go to M history` fires. Which of these happens in that tick?"
  options={[
    '`J` becomes active again, but its entry action does not run: it is only resumed',
    '`J` becomes active again and the locals of `M` keep the values they had when `M` was left',
    "`M` starts from its initial state, because history only works for strong aborts",
    "`J` is entered again: its entry action runs, and `M`'s local variables are initialised again",
  ]}
  answer={3}
>
Resuming with history is an entry like any other: `M` and `J` are entered, their entry actions run and
their locals are initialised. Only the choice of the inner state comes from history.
</Quiz>

<Quiz
  question="A chart has a superstate `On` that is entered only by `if power go to On`. Inside `On`, a transition `if menu go to Settings history` leads to a nested superstate `Settings`. What does KIELER do with that history transition?"
  options={[

    'It rejects the chart with an error',
    'It treats it as a plain `go to Settings`, without a warning',
    'It treats it as `shallow history`',
  ]}
  answer={2}
>
KIELER processes history transitions only into states of the top-level regions, and inside states that
are themselves history targets. `On` is not one, so the history on `Settings` is dropped silently.
</Quiz>

<Quiz
  question="State `B` has `entry do n++`. The chart goes to `B` with `go to B deferred` in tick 6 and stays there. When does `n++` run?"
  options={['In tick 6', 'In tick 7', 'Never during this visit']}
  answer={2}
>
A deferred entry skips the entry actions for good. The immediate behaviour of `B` that waited (immediate
transitions, `immediate during` actions) runs in tick 7, but entry actions are not postponed.
</Quiz>

<KeyIdeas>
- `go to S history` resumes `S` in the states it was in when it was last left, at every level;
  `shallow history` resumes only the level directly inside `S`.
- Resuming is an entry: entry actions run again and local variables start again from their initial
  values. History restores states, not data.
- KIELER ignores history transitions into nested states unless every state around them is also a
  history target. Keep history targets in top-level regions.
- `go to S deferred` arrives quietly: `S`'s entry actions are skipped and its immediate behaviour waits
  a tick. Use it when the inputs of the arrival tick still belong to the job just finished. History
  does not record a deferred entry.
- For a superstate, whether a deferred entry also delays the inside depends on whether all of its
  incoming transitions are deferred.
</KeyIdeas>
