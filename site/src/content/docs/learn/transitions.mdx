Every transition answers three questions. *When* may the chart leave this state? That is the
trigger. *What* does it do on the way? Those are the effects. *Where* does it go? That is the target.
In [the previous chapter](/learn/first-chart/) each state had a single transition with a simple
trigger and one effect. Real controllers need more: conditions on numbers, several effects at once,
and states with more than one way out. This chapter covers all of that.

## Triggers

A thermostat keeps a room warm without switching the heater on and off every few seconds. It turns
the heater on when the room drops below 18 degrees, and off again only once it reaches 21. Between
the two, it keeps doing whatever it was doing:

<Sim example="learn/transitions/thermostat" caption="Compare ticks 3 and 5: the same temperature, 19 degrees, with the heater on in one and off in the other." />

| Tick | `temperature` | State after | `heating` after |
| ---: | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 20 | Idle | false |
| 2 | 17 | Heating | true |
| 3 | 19 | Heating | true |
| 4 | 21 | Idle | false |
| 5 | 19 | Idle | false |

A **trigger** is any condition you can write as a boolean expression over the chart's variables:
inputs, outputs and, later in the guide, local variables. `temperature < 18` compares an `int` input
with a number. You can combine conditions with `&&` (and), `||` (or) and `!` (not), and compare with
`==`, `!=`, `<`, `<=`, `>` and `>=`. Arithmetic works too: `if a + b > 10`. The operators behave as
they do in C; [Operators](/reference/operators/) lists them all, with their precedence.

In each tick, the chart checks the triggers of the active state's transitions, with that tick's
values. It never looks at the transitions of other states. That is why ticks 3 and 5 differ: in
tick 3 the chart is in `Heating` and waits for `temperature >= 21`, in tick 5 it is in `Idle` and
waits for `temperature < 18`, and 19 satisfies neither. The state is the chart's memory of what has
happened so far.

<Aside type="caution" title="An assignment in a trigger">
`=` assigns and `==` compares, as in C. Writing `if temperature = 17` is a syntax error, and KIELER
reports `no viable alternative at input '='`, followed by `mismatched input 'do' expecting 'to'` as
the parser loses its way. Write `if temperature == 17`.
</Aside>

## Effects

An **effect** is an assignment, written after `do`: `do heating = true`. It runs only when its
transition fires, and it can use any expression on the right-hand side: `do average = (a + b) / 2`
reads two variables and writes a third. A variable keeps its value until an effect writes it, which is
why `heating` stays true for the whole time the thermostat is in `Heating`.

A transition can also have no effect at all (`if fault go to Blocked`), or several.

### Several effects

Separate several effects with semicolons. They run **one after another, left to right**, and each one
sees the values written by the effects before it.

This chart records two readings: when `sample` is true it shifts the latest reading into `previous`
and stores the new one in `latest`. The second chart is identical except for the order of its two
effects. The guided tour feeds both the same inputs and samples twice, first a 5 and then an 8:

<Compare a="learn/transitions/sampler" b="learn/transitions/sampler-swapped" scenario="learn/transitions/sampler-compare" caption="The same two effects in a different order: only `Sampler` ever remembers an older reading." />

`reading` is 5 in tick 2 and 8 from tick 3 on. After each tick, `latest` is the same in both charts,
but `previous` is not (*Swapped* is `SamplerSwapped`):

| Tick | `sample` | `latest` | Sampler `previous` | Swapped `previous` |
| ---: | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | false | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| 2 | true | 5 | 0 | 5 |
| 3 | false | 5 | 0 | 5 |
| 4 | true | 8 | 5 | 8 |
| 5 | false | 8 | 5 | 8 |

In `Sampler`, `previous = latest` runs first and copies the old value; then `latest = reading`
overwrites `latest`. In `SamplerSwapped`, `latest = reading` runs first, so `previous = latest` copies
the value that was written a moment earlier. There is no "all at once" assignment in SCCharts: the
effects of a transition are a small sequential program, run top to bottom, all within the tick. (So
`do a = b; b = a` does not swap `a` and `b`; both end up with `b`'s old value.) In the diagram, each
effect of a transition gets its own line of the label.

Both charts also have something you haven't seen yet: their transition leads from `Waiting` back to
`Waiting`. That is a *self-loop*; it gets its own section below.

<Aside type="caution" title="Punctuation in effect lists">
A semicolon goes *between* effects, never after the last one.
`do previous = latest; latest = reading; go to Waiting` gives `no viable alternative at input 'go'`. Commas don't separate effects either:
`do previous = latest, latest = reading` gives `no viable alternative at input ','`.
</Aside>

## Several transitions out of one state: priority

A pump starts when `start` is true, and must never run while a sensor reports a `fault`. So `Idle` has
two transitions: one for the fault, one for starting. What should happen in a tick in which *both*
inputs are true?

SCCharts answers with **priority**. A state's outgoing transitions are tried in the order in which they
are written: the first one whose trigger holds fires, and the others are ignored for this tick. Their
effects do not run. In the diagram the order appears as a number in front of each label, `1:`,
`2:` and so on. (KIELER only shows the numbers when a state has more than one outgoing transition.)

Here are two pumps that differ only in the order of `Idle`'s two transitions. In tick 2 of the guided
tour, `start` and `fault` both become true:

<Compare a="learn/transitions/pump" b="learn/transitions/pump-start-first" scenario="learn/transitions/pump-compare" caption="Both inputs true in tick 2: `Pump` goes straight to `Blocked`, `PumpStartFirst` runs for one tick first." />

Each chart's state after the tick (*StartFirst* is `PumpStartFirst`). In both, `pumping` is true
exactly while the pump is in `Running`:

| Tick | `start` | `fault` | Pump | StartFirst |
| ---: | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | false | false | Idle | Idle |
| 2 | true | true | Blocked | Running |
| 3 | true (held) | true (held) | Blocked | Blocked |

In `Pump` the fault check is transition 1, so in tick 2 it wins and the pump never starts. In
`PumpStartFirst` the start is transition 1: the pump starts in tick 2 despite the fault, and it takes
until tick 3 for `Running`'s own fault check to stop it.

Why not in tick 2 itself? Because `Running` was only entered in tick 2, and a state's transitions are
checked from the tick after it was entered (you saw this for the initial state in
[Thinking in ticks](/learn/ticks/)). So with the transitions you know so far, a chart moves **at most
one step per tick**: one transition fires out of the active state, and the state it enters waits for
the next tick. [Chapter 4](/learn/immediate/) shows how `immediate` transitions change that.

The rule, precisely:

- The transitions of a state have priorities 1, 2, 3, ... in the order they are written. There is no
  other way to set a priority: to change it, reorder the lines.
- In a tick, the first transition (in priority order) whose trigger holds is taken. At most one
  transition leaves a state per tick, and only the taken transition's effects run.
- If no trigger holds, nothing happens and the state stays active.

When several conditions can hold at once, put the one that must win first. Safety checks, like
`fault`, go at the top.

## Self-loops

A transition may lead back to the state it leaves: the samplers' `go to Waiting` does that. A
**self-loop** is an ordinary transition whose target happens to be its source. When it fires, the
chart **leaves the state**, runs the effects, and **enters the state again**.

With what you know so far, a re-entered state looks just like a state that stayed active. The
difference matters once states have more inside them: a re-entered state runs its entry actions
again ([chapter 10](/learn/actions/)), and it resets the variables declared inside it and restarts the
states nested inside it ([chapter 7](/learn/hierarchy/)). So use a self-loop when you mean "do this, and start this state over",
and not merely to stay put.

You never need a transition in order to stay in a state: a state with no enabled transition
stays active. A transition like this one

```sctx {2}
initial state Off
  if !button go to Off
  if button do light = true go to On
```

is not harmless decoration. From tick 2 on it fires in every tick in which `button` is false, and each
time the chart leaves `Off` and enters it again. Leave it out.

## Transitions without a trigger

A transition without a trigger, such as `go to Next` or `do x = 1 go to Next`, fires as soon as it is
allowed to: in the first tick after its state was entered, every time. You saw this in the blinker of
[Thinking in ticks](/learn/ticks/), which switched state in every tick.

Because it is always enabled, a transition without a trigger is a good **last** transition of a state:
it says "otherwise". In this stopwatch, `Running` first checks whether `stop` was pressed, and
otherwise counts one tick and loops back. Its effect `elapsed++` adds one to `elapsed`; it is one of
several assignment shorthands (`+=`, `-=` and friends) that [chapter 5](/learn/variables/) introduces
properly.

<Sim example="learn/transitions/stopwatch" caption="Watch which of `Running`'s two transitions fires: the self-loop counts, until `stop` takes priority in tick 6." />

| Tick | `start` | `stop` | State after | `elapsed` after |
| ---: | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | false | false | Stopped | 0 |
| 2 | true | false (held) | Running | 0 |
| 3 | false | false (held) | Running | 1 |
| 4 | false (held) | false (held) | Running | 2 |
| 5 | false (held) | false (held) | Running | 3 |
| 6 | false (held) | true | Stopped | 3 |
| 7 | false (held) | false | Stopped | 3 |
| 8 | true | false (held) | Running | 3 |
| 9 | false | false (held) | Running | 4 |

Three things to notice:

- In tick 2 the stopwatch starts but does not count: `Running` was entered in that tick, so its
  transitions wait until tick 3.
- From tick 3 on, the self-loop fires in every tick, and `elapsed++` counts the ticks.
- In tick 6 both of `Running`'s transitions could fire: `stop` is true, and the self-loop needs no
  trigger at all. Transition 1 wins, so the chart stops, and the count does not run.

[Chapter 10](/learn/actions/) shows a tidier way to do something in every tick while staying in a state.

## When a transition can never fire

Swap the two transitions of `Running` and the stopwatch can no longer be stopped:

```sctx {3-4}
state Running
  do elapsed++ go to Running
  if stop go to Stopped
```

The untriggered self-loop is now transition 1, and it is enabled in every tick, so transition 2 never
gets a chance. KIELER notices and warns on the `if stop` line: `The transition is not reachable.`
Try it: press `stop` in the simulation below and the count carries on.

<Sim example="learn/transitions/stopwatch-unreachable" caption="`stop` is true from tick 4, and the stopwatch keeps counting." />

<Aside type="caution" title="A default transition placed first">
`The transition is not reachable.` is only a warning, so the chart compiles and runs, but it is almost
always a bug: a transition without a trigger hides every transition after it. Move the transition
without a trigger to the end. SCCharts Lab underlines the dead transition while you type.
</Aside>

KIELER's warning covers only this case, where an earlier transition has no trigger at all. It does not
analyse your conditions. In

```sctx
initial state Start
  if a || b do choice = 1 go to Done
  if a do choice = 2 go to Done
```

the second transition can never fire either, since whenever `a` is true `a || b` is true as well, but
the chart compiles without a word. When you order transitions, put the more specific condition first.

</TryIt>

## Check your understanding

<Predict example="learn/transitions/stopwatch-predict" ask={[{ tick: 4, value: 'elapsed' }, { tick: 6, state: 'Running' }, { tick: 7, state: 'Running' }]} caption="Inputs keep their values: look closely at `stop` in ticks 6 and 7." />

<details>
<summary>Explanation</summary>

After tick 4, `elapsed` is 2: `Running` was entered in tick 2 and counted in ticks 3 and 4. In tick 5
`stop` stops it. In tick 6 `start` is true while the chart is in `Stopped`. `Stopped` only looks at
`start`, so the stopwatch starts, although `stop` is still switched on from tick 5: `Running` is
active. In tick 7 `Running` checks `stop` first, finds it still true, and stops again without
counting.

</details>

<Quiz
  question="A transition fires with the effects `do x = 1; y = x + 1; x = 5`. Before the tick, `x` and `y` are both 0. What are they after it?"
  options={['`x` is 5 and `y` is 6', '`x` is 5 and `y` is 2', '`x` is 1 and `y` is 2', '`x` is 5 and `y` is 1']}
  answer={1}
>
The effects run left to right. `x = 1` writes 1; `y = x + 1` reads that 1 and writes 2; `x = 5`
overwrites `x`. `y` is not recomputed when `x` changes later: an effect is an assignment, not an
equation.
</Quiz>

<Quiz
  question="`Idle` has two transitions: `if fault go to Blocked`, then `if start do pumping = true go to Running`. Both `fault` and `start` are true. What happens?"
  options={['Both fire: `pumping` becomes true, and the chart ends in `Blocked`', 'Neither fires, because they conflict', 'Only the first transition fires, and `pumping` stays false']}
  answer={2}
>
Transitions are tried in written order and the first enabled one wins. At most one transition leaves
a state per tick, and the effects of the others do not run, so `pumping = true` never happens.
</Quiz>

<KeyIdeas>
- A **trigger** is a boolean expression over the chart's variables. Only the active state's triggers
  count, with the current tick's values.
- An **effect** is an assignment. Several effects are separated by `;` and run left to right, each
  seeing the writes before it.
- A state's transitions are tried in written order; the first whose trigger holds fires, and only its
  effects run. The diagram numbers them when there are several.
- A transition without a trigger fires in the first tick after its state was entered. Put it last.
- A self-loop leaves its state and enters it again. To stay in a state, you need no transition.
- `The transition is not reachable.` means an untriggered transition is hiding the ones after it.
</KeyIdeas>
