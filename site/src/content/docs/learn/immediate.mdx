Every transition you have written so far has waited. A chart enters a state in one tick and can
leave it in the next tick at the earliest, which is why nothing seemed to happen in tick 1. That
rhythm keeps charts easy to follow, but some decisions belong to the moment of arrival. A lock that
has just been given a code should say yes or no straight away; a controller that starts up should
look at its inputs in tick 1, not in tick 2.

SCCharts lets you choose, one transition at a time. A **delayed** transition waits; an
**immediate** transition may fire in the same tick its state was entered. This chapter shows both,
what happens when immediate transitions follow one another within a tick, why KIELER refuses some
charts that look harmless, and how a chart comes to an end in a final state.

## Delayed transitions wait a tick

`Stairs` climbs from `Ground` to `Second`, one floor at a time, while the input `up` is true. Its
transitions are written the way you have written transitions so far. In the tour, `up` is true from
tick 1, yet the chart only reaches the top in tick 3.

<Sim example="learn/immediate/stairs" caption="`up` is true in every tick, but `floor` changes at most once per tick." />

| Tick | `up` | State after | `floor` after |
| ---: | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | true | Ground | 0 |
| 2 | true (held) | First | 1 |
| 3 | true (held) | Second | 2 |
| 4 | true (held) | Second | 2 |

Two things hold the climb up. In tick 1, `Ground` has only just been entered; in tick 2, `First` has
only just been entered. The tick in which a state is entered is that state's **entry tick**, and the
rule for an ordinary transition is short: it is never checked in its source state's entry tick. In
every later tick in which the state is still active, it is checked as usual.

Tick 1 is nothing more than the entry tick of the initial state, which is why a transition out of the
initial state can fire in tick 2 at the earliest. But the rule is about every state, whenever it is entered:
`First` is entered in tick 2, so its transition has to wait until tick 3. A chain of delayed
transitions moves at most one step per tick. Every transition without a keyword is delayed.

## Immediate transitions fire on arrival

Write `immediate` in front of a transition and it is checked in its source state's entry tick too:

```sctx
initial state Ground
  immediate if up do floor = 1 go to First
```

`Lift` is `Stairs` with both transitions immediate. Here are the two side by side, with the same
input:

<Compare a="learn/immediate/stairs" b="learn/immediate/lift" scenario="learn/immediate/stairs-lift-compare" caption="The same input and the same states: `Lift` reaches `Second` in tick 1, `Stairs` in tick 3." />

In tick 1, `Lift` enters `Ground` and checks its immediate transition at once: `up` is true, so the
chart moves to `First` and sets `floor` to 1. Entering `First` makes tick 1 `First`'s entry tick
as well, and `First`'s transition is immediate too, so it is checked straight away: the chart moves
on to `Second` and `floor` becomes 2. All of this happens within tick 1. Only the values at the end
of a tick are reported, so the 1 is never seen.

| Tick | `up` | `Stairs` after | `Lift` after |
| ---: | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | true | Ground, `floor` 0 | Second, `floor` 2 |
| 2 | true (held) | First, `floor` 1 | Second, `floor` 2 |
| 3 | true (held) | Second, `floor` 2 | Second, `floor` 2 |

### Chains of immediate transitions

A tick keeps following transitions for as long as it can: after every transition it checks the
immediate transitions of the state it has just entered, and it stops at the first state where none
of them is enabled. So one tick can pass through any number of states. Each transition's effects
run before the next transition is checked, so a trigger further along the chain sees the values
written earlier in the same tick. When you step a simulation, every transition taken in the tick is
highlighted, so a chain shows up as a path through the diagram.

The first link of a chain may be a delayed transition. When a delayed transition fires in tick 5,
its target is entered in tick 5, and the target's immediate transitions are checked in tick 5 too.
`Lock` relies on this: it waits in `Idle` until `enter` is true, then passes through `Check`, which
decides at once whether the code was right.

<Sim example="learn/immediate/lock" caption="Watch tick 2: the chart goes from `Idle` to `Check` and back to `Idle` within one tick." />

In the diagram, an immediate transition is drawn as a **dashed arrow**: `Check`'s two transitions are
dashed, and the delayed one out of `Idle` is solid.

`Check` is a state you never see at the end of a tick: it is entered and left in the same tick,
every time. Its second transition has no trigger, so it is the default when the code is wrong, and
because it is immediate as well, the chart is back in `Idle` in the same tick whichever way the
check went. That default has to come last, as in [chapter 3](/learn/transitions/#when-a-transition-can-never-fire):
written first, it would fire in every entry tick of `Check`, and KIELER would warn
`The transition is not reachable.` on the code check after it. Notice also that `enter` keeps its
value: while it stays true, `Lock` checks the code again in every tick.

## Which transitions compete

You already know that a state's outgoing transitions are checked in priority order and that the
first enabled one wins ([Triggers, effects and priority](/learn/transitions/)). Once delayed and
immediate transitions are mixed, the question is which of them take part in that contest. `Race`
has one of each: priority 1 is delayed, priority 2 is immediate.

<Sim example="learn/immediate/race" caption="`path` records which transition fired last: 1 for the delayed one, 2 for the immediate one." />

`Done` has one untriggered delayed transition back to `Choose`, so the chart keeps returning to
`Choose`: in some ticks `Choose` has just been entered, in others it was already active.

The column *Entry tick* says whether `Choose` was entered in that tick:

| Tick | `A` | `B` | Entry tick | State after | `path` after |
| ---: | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | true | true | yes | Done | 2 |
| 2 | true (held) | false | yes | Choose | 2 |
| 3 | true (held) | true | no | Done | 1 |
| 4 | true (held) | true (held) | yes | Done | 2 |
| 5 | false | false | yes | Choose | 2 |
| 6 | false (held) | true | no | Done | 2 |

Those six ticks show the whole rule:

- In a state's entry tick, only its immediate transitions are candidates, in priority order. In
  tick 1 the delayed priority 1 is skipped although `A` is true, and the immediate priority 2 fires.
  In tick 2 `B` is false, so nothing fires, and the chart stays in `Choose`.
- In every later tick, all of the state's transitions are candidates, in priority order. In tick 3
  both are enabled, and the delayed priority 1 wins. Being immediate does not make a transition go
  first.
- An immediate transition is not limited to the entry tick. In tick 6 it fires in a tick in which
  `Choose` was already active, because `A` is false.

Tick 4 is a chain that starts with a delayed transition: `Done` → `Choose` fires, `Choose` has just
been entered, so its immediate transition is checked and fires in the same tick. In short,
`immediate` changes *when* a transition may be checked; it never changes its priority.

## Immediate loops are rejected

What if a chain comes back to where it started? `Spin` tries to count to three with an immediate
self-loop.

<Chart example="learn/immediate/spin" caption="KIELER refuses to compile `Spin`: its self-loop is immediate." />

KIELER does not run this chart. What it reports:

- errors from the scheduler: one or more lines like `Can't schedule from _g1 to _cg1`, then
  `The SCG is NOT asc-schedulable!`;
- the warning `Instantaneous loop detected!`;
- on the transition itself, the warning `There is an immediate loop. The model is not SASC.`,
  followed by the states involved.

SCCharts Lab shows the same errors plus a plainer one, `Circular dependency prevents scheduling this
tick.`, and words the warning `Potential instantaneous loop.`

The reason is that a tick must end. KIELER compiles each tick into a fixed sequence of steps, and a
cycle of immediate transitions would need to go back to an earlier step, possibly for ever. You can
see that `n < 3` stops `Spin` after three rounds, but the compiler does not look at triggers: the
check is about the shape of the chart. Every cycle made only of immediate transitions is rejected,
whatever its triggers say. This one is refused too, although its two guards can never both be true:

```sctx
initial state Ping
  immediate if flip go to Pong

state Pong
  immediate if !flip go to Ping
```

The fix is always the same: make at least one transition on the cycle delayed. A delayed transition
cannot fire again in the tick its state was re-entered, so a cycle through it goes round at most
once per tick. `CountSteps` is `Spin` without the keyword `immediate`:

<Sim example="learn/immediate/count-steps" caption="One step per tick: `n` becomes 1, 2 and 3 in ticks 2, 3 and 4." />

`Lock` has a cycle as well, `Idle` → `Check` → `Idle`, and KIELER accepts it because the transition
out of `Idle` is delayed. If you really want three steps of work in one tick, don't loop: write the
result directly (`n = 3`), or spread the work over several ticks as `CountSteps` does. The chapter
[Why the compiler says no](/learn/causality/) explains the scheduling behind these messages.

<Aside>
KIELER has a second compiler, the priority-based one, which accepts loops like `Spin` and runs them
inside the tick (and never finishes the tick if the loop never stops). This guide always uses the
default netlist-based compiler, the one you get unless you pick another.
</Aside>

## Final states and termination

A state written with `final` is a **final state**; the diagram gives it a double outline. When the
chart's top level is in a final state at the end of a tick, the chart **terminates** in that tick,
and from then on it does nothing at all. (With several concurrent regions at the top level, every
one of them must be in a final state; that comes in [Concurrent regions](/learn/concurrency/).) In
this guide's timing diagrams, a small triangle next to a tick's number marks the tick in which the
chart terminated.

`Finish` counts one lap and stops. Its final state even has a transition back to `Lap`. Predict what
happens, run it, then open the explanation:

<Predict example="learn/immediate/finish" ask={[{ tick: 2, value: 'laps' }, { tick: 4, value: 'laps' }]} caption="`next` is true in ticks 1 to 3 and false in tick 4." />

<details>
<summary>Explanation</summary>

In tick 2, `Lap` → `Done` fires and `laps` becomes 1. The chart ends the tick in a final state, so it
terminates in tick 2. In tick 3, `next` is still true, and an ordinary state would now take `Done`'s
transition back to `Lap`; but a terminated chart checks nothing, so `laps` stays 1 for good.

</details>

A few details, all visible in KIELER's simulator:

- The transition into the final state runs its effects as usual; termination happens at the end of
  that tick.
- A chart can terminate in tick 1, if a chain of immediate transitions from the initial state reaches
  a final state.
- "Final" means "may count as finished", not "stop here". If a final state has an immediate
  transition that is enabled when the state is entered, the chain continues, the chart does
  not end the tick in a final state, and nothing terminates.

Most controllers never terminate: they run as long as the device is switched on. Final states earn
their keep inside superstates, where reaching one lets the superstate finish and move on with
`join to` ([Superstates](/learn/hierarchy/)). One KIELER quirk to know about already: a chart that
declares a signal never terminates in KIELER's simulator ([Signals and
termination](/learn/signals/#signals-and-termination)).

## `delayed` and `auto`

You may meet two more keywords in front of transitions. `delayed` says explicitly what no keyword
means. `auto` is a leftover that KIELER still accepts, and it also means delayed; the first example
on the [KIELER wiki's syntax page](https://github.com/kieler/semantics/wiki/Syntax) uses it.

```sctx
initial state A
  auto do step = 1 go to B

state B
  delayed do step = 2 go to C

final state C
```

Both transitions behave exactly like transitions without a keyword: this chart is in `A` after
tick 1, in `B` after tick 2 (`step` 1), and reaches `C` in tick 3, where it terminates with
`step` 2. Write transitions without a keyword, and use `immediate` where you mean it.

## Check your understanding

<Quiz
  question="A delayed transition from `Idle` to `Check` fires in tick 5. `Check` has one outgoing transition, `immediate if x > 0 go to Done`, and `x` is 3. Which state is the chart in at the end of tick 5?"
  options={['`Idle`: the chart only reaches `Check` in tick 6', '`Check`: its immediate transition fires in tick 6', '`Done`: `Check` is entered in tick 5, so its immediate transition is checked in tick 5 too']}
  answer={2}
>
Entering a state, by any transition and in any tick, makes that tick the state's entry tick, and
its immediate transitions are checked at once. The delayed transition starts the chain; the
immediate one continues it in the same tick.
</Quiz>

<Quiz
  question="`Choose` has two outgoing transitions: priority 1 is `if A go to X`, priority 2 is `immediate if B go to Y`. `Choose` was entered two ticks ago. In this tick `A` and `B` are both true. What happens?"
  options={['Priority 1 fires and the chart goes to `X`', 'Priority 2 fires, because immediate transitions go first', 'Nothing: two enabled transitions conflict']}
  answer={0}
>
Outside its entry tick, all of a state's transitions are candidates, in priority order, and the
first enabled one wins. `immediate` only matters in the entry tick, when the delayed transition
would not have been a candidate.
</Quiz>

<Quiz
  question="Which of these charts does KIELER accept?"
  options={['`A` goes to `B` with `immediate if I`, and `B` goes back to `A` with `immediate if !I`', '`A` has a self-loop `immediate if x < 3 do x += 1 go to A`', '`A` goes to `B` with `immediate if I`, and `B` goes back to `A` with `if !I`']}
  answer={2}
>
The first two contain a cycle made only of immediate transitions, which KIELER rejects whatever the
triggers say (`The SCG is NOT asc-schedulable!`). The third cycle contains a delayed transition, so
it can go round at most once per tick.
</Quiz>

<KeyIdeas>
- A state's **entry tick** is the tick in which it is entered. Delayed transitions (no keyword,
  `delayed` or `auto`) are not checked in it; immediate transitions are.
- After the entry tick, all of a state's transitions compete in priority order. `immediate` never
  raises a transition's priority.
- A tick follows immediate transitions until it reaches a state where none is enabled. A delayed
  transition can start such a chain.
- KIELER rejects every cycle made only of immediate transitions, whatever the guards. Make one
  transition on the cycle delayed.
- When the top level is in a final state at the end of a tick, the chart terminates and does
  nothing from then on.
</KeyIdeas>
