Press cancel on a printer, and one of two things can happen: it finishes the sheet it is printing
and then stops, or it stops dead. Both are right in their place. A cancelled job should not leave
half a sheet in the printer; a paper jam has to stop the rollers at once, whatever they were about to
do.

SCCharts has a transition for each. You met the first kind in [Superstates](/learn/hierarchy/): a
transition written after a superstate's closing brace leaves the superstate from whichever state is
active inside it. Such a transition is an **abort**, and it comes in two strengths: the `go to` you know
is the weak one, and ABRO's reset at the end of [Concurrent regions](/learn/concurrency/),
`if R abort to ABO`, is the strong one.
(Aborts and suspension, which comes in [Going further](/advanced/suspend/), together make up
**preemption**: something outside a state taking control away from it.) This chapter is about the
tick in which a superstate is left: what its inside may still do, and which way out wins when several
apply at once.

## Weak and strong

Here is a print job twice, once ended by `go to` and once by `abort to`. Every sheet that comes out
of the printer is counted, and `cancel` ends the job.

<Compare a="learn/preemption/weak" b="learn/preemption/strong" scenario="learn/preemption/weak-vs-strong" caption="In tick 5 `cancel` and a sheet arrive together: `WeakCancel` counts the sheet, `StrongCancel` does not." />

The tour starts the job in tick 2, sends a sheet in every tick from tick 3 and cancels in tick 5.
KIELER gives:

| Tick | `sheet` | `cancel` | `printed`, weak | `printed`, strong |
| ---: | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 3 | true | false (held) | 1 | 1 |
| 4 | true (held) | false (held) | 2 | 2 |
| 5 | true (held) | true | 3 | 2 |

In tick 5 the two kinds of abort part ways:

- A **weak abort**, written `go to`, is the default. In the tick it fires, the inside of the
  superstate first completes its reaction: its transitions fire, chains of immediate transitions run
  to their end. Only then is the superstate left. `WeakCancel` counts the sheet of tick 5.
- A **strong abort**, written `abort to`, leaves the superstate before anything inside it reacts in
  that tick. `StrongCancel` never sees the sheet of tick 5.

Either way, the abort's own effect runs next, and then its target is entered. In the diagram KIELER
marks a strong abort with a red circle where it starts; a weak abort has no mark.

Which one to use is a question of what the inside may still do. Use a strong abort when nothing
inside may act any more once the trigger is there: an emergency stop, a reset that has to win. Use a
weak abort when the inside should finish what it is doing in this tick: complete a step, let a last
output out. A weak abort grants the inside a last wish.

States can also carry actions of their own, which run when a state is entered, while it is active
and when it is left. What an abort does to them is part of the next chapter,
[Entry, during and exit actions](/learn/actions/).

## Delayed and immediate aborts

An abort is a transition, and the rule from [Delayed and immediate transitions](/learn/immediate/)
applies to it: without `immediate`, it is not checked in the tick its superstate is entered.
Usually that is what you want. It makes a difference when the trigger is already there as the
superstate is entered:

<Compare a="learn/preemption/delayed-abort" b="learn/preemption/immediate-abort" scenario="learn/preemption/abort-entry" caption="`start` arrives in tick 2 while `cancel` is held: `DelayedAbort` stays in `Job` for that tick, `ImmediateAbort` is back in `Idle` at once." />

With `immediate`, the abort is checked in the entry tick and in every tick after it. If it fires in
the entry tick, the superstate is entered and left in that one tick. For a weak abort, the immediate
transitions inside the superstate still run first; a strong abort skips them.

## When the trigger is read

A strong abort is decided before the inside reacts, a weak abort after it. That matters when the
inside itself changes the abort's trigger. This print job ends by itself once three sheets are out:

<Compare a="learn/preemption/tray-weak" b="learn/preemption/tray-strong" scenario="learn/preemption/tray-compare" caption="Tick 5 counts the third sheet: `TrayWeak` leaves in that tick, `TrayStrong` one tick later." />

In tick 5 the third sheet makes `printed` 3. The weak abort reads its trigger after the inside has
reacted, sees 3, and leaves at once. The strong abort read its trigger before the inside reacted,
when `printed` was still 2, so it fires one tick later, in tick 6, and skips that tick's sheet.

*Strong looks before the inside moves; weak looks after.* To leave a superstate because of something
its inside did, use a weak abort or, better, a final state and `join to`. Avoid strong aborts whose
trigger is written by the state they abort: with several regions inside, KIELER then aborts the
regions one by one, some a tick later than others, and some such charts are rejected as causality
cycles. [Watchdogs](/patterns/watchdog/#keep-the-watchdog-outside) shows both outcomes, and the
arrangement that avoids them.

## Several ways out

A superstate can have several outgoing transitions. As for any state, they are tried in the order
written, and the first whose trigger holds wins (see
[Triggers, effects and priority](/learn/transitions/)). Aborts add one rule: **strong aborts come
first.** KIELER rejects any state, simple states included, in which a strong abort is written after a
weak abort or a termination transition.

<Chart example="learn/preemption/order-error" caption="`jam` is a strong abort, written below the weak `cancel`. KIELER rejects the chart." />

The fix is to move every `abort to` of a state above its `go to` and `join to` transitions.

The rule follows from the timing in the previous section. A strong abort has to be decided before
the inside reacts; a weak abort or a join can only be decided after. A weak abort with a higher
priority than a strong one would have to be decided first, so it would need the inside's reaction
before the strong abort had its chance to prevent that reaction. KIELER calls that a causality
problem and refuses.

With the order right, a strong and a weak abort that hold in the same tick are easy to decide: the
strong one comes first, so it wins, and nothing inside runs.

## Finishing versus being stopped

A termination transition is the third way out of a superstate. It is not an abort: it leaves because
the inside has finished, not because something outside says so. In its timing it behaves like a weak
abort, though. The inside reacts first in the tick, and the join fires after it.

Now suppose the inside finishes in the same tick in which a weak abort fires. Both ways out apply,
and the order decides:

<Compare a="learn/preemption/two-pages" b="learn/preemption/two-pages-join-first" scenario="learn/preemption/two-pages-compare" caption="In tick 4 both jobs finish and are cancelled. In each chart the transition written first wins." />

In both charts the second sheet is counted in tick 4 (both ways out are weak, so the inside reacts
first), and `Job` has both finished and been cancelled. `TwoPages` writes the cancel first and emits
`cancelled`; `TwoPagesJoinFirst` writes the join first and emits `finished`. Put first the transition
that should win.

To leave a superstate when its inside is done, use `join to`, not a `go to` without a trigger. On a
superstate, an abort without a trigger only gets the warning `Abort transitions should have a
trigger.`, and it fires in the tick after the superstate was entered, whatever the inside is doing.

## Aborts on simple states

A transition out of a simple state counts as an abort too, but a simple state has no inside to finish
or to skip. For a simple state without actions, `go to` and `abort to` behave exactly alike; KIELER
turns such strong aborts into weak ones. A state with a `during` action is the one case where they
differ, and the next chapter shows it.

## ABRO, the classic

Back to ABRO from [Concurrent regions](/learn/concurrency/). Its last line, `if R abort to ABO`, is a
strong abort from `ABO` back to itself. Such a self-loop is a full exit and re-entry: `ABO` is left
and entered again from its initial state, so every region inside starts over.

Why strong? Look at the tick in which `R` arrives together with the last of `A` and `B`. ABRO's
specification says that `R` starts again from the beginning, so that `B` should not complete the old
round. Here is ABRO next to a copy whose reset is weak:

<Compare a="learn/preemption/abro" b="learn/preemption/abro-weak" scenario="learn/preemption/abro-compare" caption="`A` comes in tick 2, then `B` and `R` together in tick 4: the strong reset suppresses `O`; with the weak one the inside finishes first, and `O` is present." />

With `abort to`, `ABO` is left before `HandleB` sees `B`: no join, no `O`, and in tick 4 the new
round starts with both regions waiting. That `B` does not count for the new round either: `WaitB` was
entered in tick 4, and its delayed transition cannot fire in its entry tick. With `go to`, the last
wish lets `HandleB` finish, `WaitAB` terminates and emits `O`, and only then is `ABO` restarted.

Which behaviour is right is a matter of specification. The classic answer is the strong one: `R`
wins.

KIELER compiles `ABROWeak` with a warning, `Instantaneous loop detected!` (SCCharts Lab:
`Potential instantaneous loop.`). KIELER gives this warning for many charts in which a weak abort leads
straight back into its own superstate; the chart still compiles and behaves as described here
([more about this warning](/reference/errors/#instantaneous-loop-detected)).

<Aside type="danger" title="Immediate waits break ABRO in KIELER">
ABRO with immediate waits, `immediate if A go to DoneA`, so that an `A` in the tick of a reset counts,
compiles with only the instantaneous-loop warning, but KIELER does not run it as written: with `A`
and `B` both in tick 1 there is no `O`, and the chart never reacts again, not even to `R`. Keep the
waits delayed; [The immediate variant](/patterns/abro/#the-immediate-variant) shows a version with
flags that works.
</Aside>

`TwoPagesStrong` is `TwoPages` with a strong cancel, written first as it must be. In tick 4 the second
sheet and `cancel` arrive together.

<details>
<summary>Explanation</summary>

`printed` is 1, `finished` is absent and `cancelled` is present. The strong cancel is decided before
anything inside `Job` reacts, so the second sheet of tick 4 is not counted, `Job` never reaches `Done`,

Strong aborts must come before every weak abort and termination transition of the same state.
Written after a `go to`, the `abort to` makes KIELER reject the chart:

```text wrap
Causality problem!
Strong abort transitions must have a higher priority than weak abort or termination transitions.
```
</Quiz>

>
A simple state has no inside, so there is nothing for a strong abort to skip: KIELER treats strong
aborts of simple states without actions as weak ones. With a `during` action the two would differ in
the leaving tick, as the next chapter shows.
</Quiz>

<KeyIdeas>
- A transition written on a superstate is an abort: it can cut the inside short. A weak abort
  (`go to`) lets the inside finish its tick first; a strong abort (`abort to`) leaves before the
  inside moves.
- Aborts are delayed unless written `immediate`. A strong abort reads its trigger before the inside
  reacts, a weak abort after.
- Transitions are tried in the order written, and strong aborts must come first. A join behaves like
  a weak abort; when a join and a weak abort both apply, the first written wins.
- On a simple state there is no inside to skip: `go to` and `abort to` behave alike unless the state
  has a `during` action.
</KeyIdeas>
