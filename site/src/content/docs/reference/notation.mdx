You never draw an SCChart by hand: you write the text, and KIELER lays out and draws the diagram for
you. Each construct has its own look, and once you know the symbols you can read a chart's
behaviour off its diagram. Every diagram on this page is a real chart, drawn the way KIELER and
SCCharts Lab draw it, except that [dataflow regions](#dataflow-regions) are simplified; switch to
**Code** to see the text behind it.

## The chart itself

The chart is the outermost box, with its name at the top. Below the name come its declarations, one
line per declaration with the keywords in colour (`input bool A, B`), then its own actions, and then
the region that holds its states. Every region has a small grey corner at its top left; in KIELER it
collapses the region to save space (in this guide's diagrams it is only drawn).

## States

<Chart example="reference/notation/states" caption="In the region `Steps`, `First` is initial (heavy outline), `Middle` ordinary and `Last` final (double outline); `Ready`, in the region `Idle`, is initial and final at once." />

- An **ordinary state** is a rounded box with its name, drawn with a thin outline.
- The **initial state** of a region, where the region starts, has a heavy outline. Each region has
  exactly one.
- A **final state** has a double outline. When every region of a superstate is in a final state, the
  superstate has finished; at the top level, the chart terminates.
- A state that is both (`initial final state`) has both: a double outline whose outer ring is heavy.
  Its region has finished as soon as it starts, so here the chart terminates as soon as `Last` is
  reached.

A state with a label (`state Off "Light off"`) shows the label instead of its name; see
[labels](#labels-and-comments).

## Transitions

<Chart example="reference/notation/transitions" caption="Three transitions leave `Wait`, numbered by priority; the one out of `Fast` is dashed because it is immediate." />

A transition is an arrow from its source state to its target state. What it says is written on the
arrow:

- **Solid or dashed.** A solid line is a delayed transition, the default: it cannot fire in the tick
  its source state is entered. A dashed line is an immediate transition, which can.
- **Priority.** When a state has more than one outgoing transition, each label starts with its
  priority, `1:`, `2:` and so on, in the order the transitions are written. In any tick the enabled
  transition with the lowest number wins. A state with a single outgoing transition shows no number.
- **Trigger and effects.** The trigger comes first (`A && B`); the effects follow a `/`, one per line
  (`/ x = 3`). A transition without a trigger shows only its effects (`/ x++`); one with neither has no
  label at all, like the arrow from `Both` back to `Wait`.
- **Count delay.** `2 B` means "the second tick in which `B` holds", written `if 2 B`.
- **Self-loop.** An arrow from a state back to itself, like `3: 2 B`. Taking it leaves the state and
  enters it again.

The warning under the chart is one of KIELER's
[false alarms](/reference/errors/#instantaneous-loop-detected): it suspects an instantaneous loop, but
compiles and runs the chart as drawn.

## Superstates and regions

<Chart example="reference/notation/superstate" caption="The superstate `Counter` shows its declaration and entry action under its name, and its states in the inner area." />

A **superstate** is a state with an inside. It is drawn as a large box: its name at the top, then its
declarations and actions, then an inner area (a region) with its own states and transitions. The
arrow from the edge of `Counter` back to itself belongs to the superstate, not to the state inside.
Such a reset is a typical cause of the same harmless loop warning, shown under the chart.

<Chart example="reference/notation/regions" caption="`WaitAB` has two concurrent regions; the first shows its label, *Wait for A*, the second its name, `HandleB`." />

A superstate with several **concurrent regions** shows them side by side, each in its own area with
its name or label in the top corner. All of them are active at once. A region written without a name
and label shows nothing there. A [for region](/advanced/for-regions/) is drawn once, with its range
after its name: `region Seat for i : 0 to 2` shows `Seat | i [ 0, 2 ]`.

## Leaving a superstate

The marker at the start of an arrow tells you how it leaves a superstate:

<Chart example="reference/notation/preemption" caption="From top: a strong abort (red dot), a weak abort (no marker) and a termination (green triangle, dashed because it is immediate)." />

- **No marker: weak abort** (`go to`). When the trigger holds, the inside of the superstate still
  completes its tick, and then the superstate is left.
- **Red dot: strong abort** (`abort to`). When the trigger holds, the superstate is left before the
  inside does anything in that tick.
- **Green triangle: termination** (`join to`). Taken when every region inside has reached a final
  state. A termination without a trigger is immediate, so it is drawn dashed.

[Aborts](/learn/preemption/) explains exactly what the inside may still do in each case, and
[Entry, during and exit actions](/learn/actions/#actions-and-aborts) which actions run.

## Returning to a superstate

Markers at the head of an arrow say how the target superstate is entered:

<Chart example="reference/notation/history" caption="The four arrows back into `Mode`, from top: deep history (H*), shallow history (H), deferred (red dot) and deep deferred (red dot with a star)." />

- **H\*: deep history** (`go to S history`). The superstate resumes in the states it was in when it was
  left, at every level.
- **H: shallow history** (`go to S shallow history`). Only the superstate's own regions resume; states
  nested deeper start again from their initial states.
- **Red dot: deferred** (`go to S deferred`). The target is entered quietly: its entry actions do not
  run, and its immediate transitions wait until the next tick. A star in the dot marks
  `deep deferred`, which treats the inner initial states the same way.
- **No marker** (or `reset`): the superstate starts from its initial states.

KIELER honours history only for superstates at the top level of a chart (and inside superstates that
are themselves entered with history); elsewhere it silently restarts the superstate, although the
marker is drawn. The [history chapter](/advanced/history/) shows this.

A marker at the start of an arrow is about leaving its source; a marker at the head is about entering
its target. One arrow can carry both.

## Connector states

<Chart example="reference/notation/connector" caption="The small filled dot is the connector state, called `Classify` in the code: control passes through it and takes the first branch whose trigger holds." />

A **connector state** is drawn as a small filled dot, without a name. It is a junction, not a place to
wait: its outgoing transitions are always immediate, and drawn dashed, even without the keyword
`immediate`, and control passes through it in the same tick. KIELER still asks you to write the
keyword, with the warning
[`Outgoing transitions of connector states should be marked as immediate.`](/reference/errors/#outgoing-transitions-of-connector-states-should-be-marked-as-immediate)
Give the last branch no trigger, so that one branch always fires.

## Actions

<Chart example="reference/notation/actions" caption="`Busy` lists its actions under its name, one per line, each starting with its keyword." />

A state's actions are listed inside its box, under the name: the keyword (`entry`, `during`,
`immediate during`, `exit`), then the trigger if there is one, then `/` and the effects. The other
actions are listed the same way: `suspend if hold` appears as `suspend hold` (the trigger without its
`if`), `weak suspend hold` and `immediate suspend hold` likewise; `period 100` is listed as written. A
state with actions is drawn as a box rather than a capsule, even when it has no inner states.

## References

<Chart example="reference/notation/reference" caption="`Blinking` is an instance of the chart `Blinker`, with `tick` and `lamp` connected to its interface." />

A state that is an instance of another chart (`state Blinking is Blinker(tick to tick, lamp to light)`)
is drawn with a violet fill and the title `Blinking @ Blinker (tick, lamp)`: the state's name, the
referenced chart, and the variables passed to it. Its inside is collapsed (the `+` corner); KIELER can
expand it to show the referenced chart in place.

## Dataflow regions

<Chart example="reference/notation/dataflow" caption="Inputs on the left, operators in the middle, outputs on the right: `O = A && !B` and `N = I * 2 + 1`." />

A dataflow region is drawn as a circuit: the variables an equation reads come in on the left, each
operator is a small box, constants are square boxes, and the variables it writes leave on the right.

<Aside type="note">
This guide draws dataflow regions as a simplified circuit. KIELER's own drawing of the same region is
built from the same parts but differs in detail; the rest of the notation on this page matches
KIELER's.
</Aside>

## Labels and comments

<Chart example="reference/notation/labels" caption="The states show their labels instead of their names, the region its label, and the transitions *press* and *press again* instead of their triggers and effects." />

Labels replace names and expressions in the diagram, never the behaviour:

- `state Off "Light off"` shows *Light off* instead of `Off`; `region Main "The lamp":` shows its
  label in the region's corner.
- `go to On label "press"` shows *press* instead of the trigger and effects. KIELER adds a hint to the
  transition, `The trigger of this action is hidden by the label.`, because the label can hide what
  really happens.

<Chart example="reference/notation/comments" caption="Doc comments become yellow notes attached to the chart and to the state `Off`." />

A **doc comment**, `/** … */` directly before the chart, a state or a region, is shown as a yellow
note attached to it; one before a transition becomes an extra label on its arrow. KIELER shows these
notes by default (its diagram option *Comment Nodes*), and so do this guide's diagrams. The annotation
`@diagram[commentNodes] false` before `scchart` turns them off for one chart. Ordinary comments (`//`,
`/* … */`) never appear in the diagram.

## In a simulation

When a chart runs, the diagram shows where it is. KIELER, SCCharts Lab and this guide use the same
two colours: **red** for the states that are active after the tick, **blue** for the transitions taken
in it and the states that were left. In this guide's simulations no mark relies on colour alone:

- An **active state** has a heavy red outline with a glow and its name in bold; a simple state is
  also tinted pale red.
- A **transition that fired** is drawn heavier, in blue, with a glow, and a short pulse runs along it
  once as it fires (not when your system asks for reduced motion). It keeps its own line: solid when
  delayed, dashed when immediate, so you can still tell which it is.
- A **state that was left** during the tick, or entered and left again within it, gets a blue outline
  and a pale blue tint that fades. A state entered in the tick glows briefly.

The guide's diagrams also mark compiler messages: in a chart that KIELER rejects, the states and
transitions a **message is about** get a solid red-orange outline with a glow and a small red-orange
disc with an exclamation mark.

None of these marks adds dashes: on a transition, a dashed line always means that it is immediate.

<Sim example="reference/notation/simulation" caption="Step through the tour: in tick 2 two transitions fire at once, and the active state changes from `Wait` to `Done`." />

In tick 2, `go1` is true: the delayed transition out of `Wait` fires, `Fast` is entered, and its
immediate transition fires straight away, so both arrows are marked, one solid and one dashed, and
the chart ends the tick in `Done`. `Fast` was active only for a moment within the tick, so it is never
outlined in red; it gets the blue outline of a state that was left, and so does `Wait`.
