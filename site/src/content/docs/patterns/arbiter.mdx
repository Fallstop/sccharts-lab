Two computers share one printer. Either may send a job at any moment, and sometimes both do in the
same tick, but the printer can only print one job at a time. Something has to decide who goes first,
make sure the printer is never given to both, and pass it on when a job is finished. That something is
an **arbiter**. The same problem turns up wherever a device is shared: two machines and one robot arm,
two tasks and one serial port, two sensors and one radio.

This page builds an arbiter for the printer. It starts with the chart that does not work, then gives
the printer away with a fixed priority, then lets the computers take turns with `pre`. Every arbiter
has to say what happens when both clients ask in the same tick, and that is the tick the questions on
this page are about.

## Two computers, one printer

The obvious chart gives each computer a region of its own. A computer that wants to print writes its
number into `user`, the variable that says whose job the printer is printing:

<Chart example="patterns/arbiter/clash" caption="Each computer's region writes `user`. KIELER rejects the chart." />

This is the write–write conflict from [Why the compiler says no](/learn/causality/#writewrite-conflicts):
two concurrent regions assign different values to the same variable, `user = 1` and `user = 2`.
Official KIELER reports `Can't schedule from …` (several times) and `The SCG is NOT asc-schedulable!`;
SCCharts Lab adds the readable version:

```text wrap
Circular dependency involving user prevents scheduling this tick.
```

The compiler is right to refuse, and not only for its own reasons. Nothing in this chart stops both
computers from printing at once: if both ask in the same tick, both regions move to `Printing`, and
`user` can only name one of them.

The cure is the one from [Latches](/patterns/latch/#one-writer-per-variable): give `user` one writer.
That writer is the arbiter. The computers no longer take the printer; they ask for it, and the arbiter
decides. (The other usual cure for a write–write conflict, updates such as `user += 1` in both regions,
does not fit here. It suits a variable that adds up what several regions contribute, such as a count of
jobs; `user` names one computer, and adding numbers cannot choose between them.)

## A fixed-priority arbiter

For now the computers are outside the chart. `reqA` is true while computer A has a job waiting, `reqB`
the same for B, and the printer reports `done` when it has finished a job. The arbiter has one state
for each answer to "who has the printer?", and `user` gives the same answer as a code: 0 for nobody, 1
for A, 2 for B (an [enum](/advanced/data-types/#enums-names-instead-of-codes) could give the codes
names):

<Sim example="patterns/arbiter/fixed-priority" caption="Ticks 2 to 6: one job each, handed over in the tick the first one finishes. From tick 7 both computers keep sending jobs: watch who gets the printer in ticks 9, 11 and 13." />

Three things make this chart an arbiter:

- **One state, one user.** The arbiter is in exactly one of `Free`, `PrintingA` and `PrintingB`, so it
  cannot give the printer to both computers. You don't have to check that; it follows from the shape
  of the chart. And `user` has one writer, so KIELER accepts the chart.
- **No idle ticks.** `Free`'s transitions are immediate. A job that arrives while the printer is free
  starts in the same tick (tick 2). When a job finishes, `PrintingA` goes back to `Free` and `Free`
  passes the printer on, all in the tick of `done` (tick 4).
- **A rule for the tie.** When both computers have a job waiting (tick 7), both of `Free`'s transitions
  are enabled, and the one written first wins. The diagram numbers them `1` and `2`: they are
  [priorities](/learn/transitions/#several-transitions-out-of-one-state-priority). A has the higher one,
  always, so this is a **fixed-priority** arbiter.

Fixed priority is simple, and sometimes it is exactly what you want: an emergency stop should always
beat a normal command. But look at ticks 9, 11 and 13. Every time A's job finishes, A has
another one waiting, and A wins again. B has been waiting since tick 7, and will go on waiting for as
long as A keeps sending jobs. A client that never gets its turn **starves**.

## Taking turns: round robin

A fair arbiter lets the computers take turns when both are waiting. This is a **round-robin** arbiter,
and it needs to know who had the printer last. In the tick in which a job finishes, `pre(user)` says
exactly that: it is the value of `user` at the end of the previous tick, while the job was still
printing (see [The previous tick](/learn/pre/)). Only A's trigger changes:

```sctx {2}
initial state Free
  immediate if reqA && (!reqB || pre(user) != 1) do user = 1 go to PrintingA
  immediate if reqB do user = 2 go to PrintingB
```

A gets the printer if it is asking and either B is not asking, or A did not have the printer a moment
ago. Otherwise B's transition gets its chance. Here are both arbiters with the same inputs:

<Compare a="patterns/arbiter/fixed-priority" b="patterns/arbiter/round-robin" scenario="b" caption="Ticks 9, 11 and 13: `FixedPriority` gives the printer to A every time, `RoundRobin` gives it to each computer in turn." />

**Fixed** and **Round robin** are `user` in `FixedPriority` and in `RoundRobin`:

| Tick | `reqA` | `reqB` | `done` | Fixed | Round robin |
| ---: | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 7 | true | true | false | 1 | 1 |
| 9 | true (held) | true (held) | true | 1 | 2 |
| 11 | true (held) | true (held) | true | 1 | 1 |
| 13 | true (held) | true (held) | true | 1 | 2 |

`RoundRobin` never lets a computer starve: a computer that is waiting when the other's job finishes
always goes next. The trigger has to read `pre(user)`, not `user`. In the tick of `done`, the
transition back to `Free` has just set `user = 0`, so when `Free`'s triggers are checked, `user` no
longer says who was printing.

### One tick of memory

`pre` looks back exactly one tick. What happens when a job finishes with nobody waiting, and a tick
later both computers send a job at once? Predict, check, then open the explanation:

<Predict
  example="patterns/arbiter/round-robin-predict"
  ask={[{ tick: 5, value: 'user' }, { tick: 7, value: 'user' }]}
  caption="`RoundRobin`. A's job prints from tick 2 and finishes in tick 4, with nobody waiting. In tick 5 both computers send a job, and in tick 7 that job finishes while both are still waiting. Who prints after ticks 5 and 7?"
/>

<details>
<summary>Explanation</summary>

After tick 5 A prints (`user` is 1), after tick 7 B (`user` is 2). In tick 4 A's job finished with
nobody waiting, so `user` went back to 0 and was still 0 at the end of the tick. In tick 5 `pre(user)`
is that 0, not A's 1. The arbiter has forgotten that A printed last, A's trigger holds, and A wins the
tie although it was B's turn. In tick 7 `pre(user)` is 1, and B gets the printer.

</details>

No computer starves because of this, but a tie after an idle tick always goes to A. To remember the last
user for longer, keep it in a variable of its own; that is the second exercise below.

## Clients inside the chart

The computers need not be outside the chart. In `PrintRoom` each computer is a region that remembers
its job, as the call button in [Latches](/patterns/latch/) remembers a call, and asks for the printer
with a local signal until its job starts. The arbiter is the third region, and the only one that writes
`user`:

<Sim
  example="patterns/arbiter/print-room"
  show={['jobA', 'jobB', 'done', 'user', 'reqA', 'startA', 'reqB', 'startB']}
  caption="Tick 2: the request, the decision and the start of the job happen in one tick, across three regions."
/>

The regions talk in both directions within one tick. `ComputerA` emits `reqA` in `Waiting`'s immediate
during action; `Arbiter` tests `reqA`, writes `user` and emits `startA`; `ComputerA`'s immediate
transition tests `startA` and goes back to `Idle`. KIELER finds an order for that, every emission of a
signal before every test of it, as in [Handshakes](/patterns/handshake/#a-handshake-within-one-tick),
and accepts the chart.

Both uses of `immediate` matter. Without the one on the during action, a computer starts asking only in the
tick after its job arrives, and every job starts a tick late. Without the one on the transition out of
`Waiting`, a computer misses `startA`, which is present only in the tick it is emitted: it keeps
asking, and when the printer is next free its job is printed a second time. (Each computer holds one
job at a time here; a real print queue would count them.)

## Try it

<TryIt example="patterns/arbiter/three-starter" solution="patterns/arbiter/three" title="A third computer">
The starter declares a third computer, C (`reqC`, and `user` 3 while C prints), but never gives it the
printer. Give C the lowest priority. To test it: C's job alone in tick 2 starts at once; with all
three waiting from tick 3, the printer goes to A in tick 4, to B in tick 6 (A has stopped sending), and
to C in tick 8.
</TryIt>

<TryIt example="patterns/arbiter/remember-starter" solution="patterns/arbiter/remember" title="Remember who went last">
`RoundRobin` forgets who printed last once the printer has been free for a tick. Give the arbiter a
local variable `last` that keeps the last user until the next job starts, and use it instead of
`pre(user)`, so that a tie after an idle tick goes to the computer that did not print last. To test it:
A's job prints from tick 2 and finishes in tick 4 with nobody waiting; both computers send a job in
tick 5 (B should win) and keep sending; when that job finishes in tick 7, A should get the printer.
</TryIt>

## Check your understanding

<Predict
  example="patterns/arbiter/fixed-priority-predict"
  ask={[{ tick: 1, value: 'user' }, { tick: 3, value: 'user' }, { tick: 5, value: 'user' }]}
  caption="`FixedPriority`. Both computers have a job in tick 1. In tick 3 A's job finishes, and A sends another one in the same tick; in tick 5 that one finishes, with only B waiting. Who prints after ticks 1, 3 and 5?"
/>

<details>
<summary>Explanation</summary>

A prints after ticks 1 and 3 (`user` is 1), B after tick 5 (`user` is 2). `Free`'s transitions are
immediate, so they are checked in tick 1 already, and A's comes first. In tick 3 both requests are
there when `Free` decides: that B has been waiting since tick 1 makes no difference to a fixed
priority. In tick 5 only B is waiting, and B prints at last.

</details>

<Quiz
  question="How do you give computer B the higher priority in `FixedPriority`?"
  options={["Make B's transition delayed instead of immediate", "Swap the order of `Free`'s two transitions", "Add `&& !reqA` to the end of B's trigger"]}
  answer={1}
>
The transition written first has priority 1, so swapping the two makes B win every tie. A delayed B
transition still comes second, and it is not checked in the tick `Free` is entered: B would still lose
every tie, and wait a tick longer each time the printer is handed over to it. `&& !reqA` changes
nothing: B's trigger is only checked when A's transition has not fired, and then `reqA` is false
anyway.
</Quiz>

<Quiz
  question="What happens if `RoundRobin`'s first trigger reads `user != 1` instead of `pre(user) != 1`?"
  options={['It behaves like `FixedPriority`: A wins every tie', 'Nothing changes: in `Free`, `user` and `pre(user)` are the same', 'KIELER rejects the chart: the transition reads and writes `user`']}
  answer={0}
>
Whenever `Free`'s triggers are checked, `user` is 0: it starts at 0, and every transition back to
`Free` sets it to 0. So `user != 1` always holds, and A's trigger comes down to `reqA`, as in
`FixedPriority`. KIELER accepts the chart: a region may read a variable and then write it, in its own
order.
</Quiz>

<KeyIdeas>
- An arbiter is the one writer of the variable that says who has the device; the clients send it
  requests. Concurrent clients that write that variable themselves are rejected.
- One state per user makes "never two at once" a property of the chart's shape.
- Decide the tie. The first of a state's transitions wins, which gives a fixed priority, and a fixed
  priority can starve the client with the lower one.
- Round robin: when both ask, the one that did not have the device last goes first. `pre(user)` says
  who had it a tick ago; a variable remembers for longer.
- Immediate transitions out of `Free` start a job in the tick it arrives and hand the device over in the
  tick it is released.
</KeyIdeas>
