A request is often over long before anyone can act on it. You press a lift's call button for a fraction
of a second; the lift may take half a minute to come. Something has to remember that the button was
pressed, show it (the lamp in the button), and forget it again once the lift has arrived. That
something is a **latch**: it is *set* by a request and *reset* by the service.

This page builds a latch for a lift call button, looks at the two questions every latch has to answer
(who wins when a request and its service arrive in the same tick, and who may write the remembered
value), and shows the same latch as a one-line equation.

## A latch with two states

The memory is a state. `Idle` means "no call", `Waiting` means "a call is registered". The lamp is on
exactly while `Waiting` is active, thanks to its entry and exit actions:

<Sim example="patterns/latch/call-button" caption="The press in tick 2 lasts one tick; the lamp stays on until the lift arrives in tick 7. Then watch ticks 8 and 9." />

The press in tick 2 lasts a single tick, but the chart stays in `Waiting` until `arrived`, so the lamp
stays on. A second press while waiting (tick 5) changes nothing, because `Waiting` has no transition that
looks at the button. That is usually what you want: a lift call is either registered or it is not.

Ticks 8 and 9 show a flaw. The lift is standing at the floor with its doors open when somebody presses
the button. `Idle` sees the press and registers a call, and in the next tick, the doors still being open,
`Waiting` serves it again. The lamp flashes for one tick, for a call that never needed registering.

## When the request and the service coincide

Every latch has to decide what happens when the set and the reset arrive together. There are two
places where they can meet:

- **In `Idle`**: a press while the lift is already here. The press is served at once, so it should not
  be registered: `if button && !arrived go to Waiting`.
- **In `Waiting`**: the lift arrives in the very tick someone presses again. The lift is here, so this
  press is served together with the call that was waiting: `if arrived go to Idle` stays as it is.

<Compare a="patterns/latch/call-button" b="patterns/latch/call-button-fixed" scenario="b" caption="Tick 8: a press while the doors are open. Only the fixed version leaves the lamp off." />

**First** and **Fixed** are `lamp` in `CallButton` and in `CallButtonFixed`:

| Tick | Inputs | First | Fixed |
| ---: | --- | --- | --- |
| 7 | `arrived` | false | false |
| 8 | `button`, `arrived` (held) | true | false |
| 9 | `arrived` (held) | false | false |
| 13 | `button`, `arrived` | false | false |

Here the reset wins in both places. That is right for a lift, but it is a decision, not a law. A latch
whose reset wins is called **reset-dominant**; one whose set wins is **set-dominant**.

### A latch where the set must win

Think of an alarm that a fault switches on and an operator acknowledges with `ack`. If the operator
acknowledges while the fault is still present, a reset-dominant latch switches the alarm off, and the
still-present fault switches it straight back on in the next tick. The acknowledgement is wasted, and the
alarm flickers. A set-dominant latch refuses the acknowledgement for as long as the fault lasts:

<Compare a="patterns/latch/alarm-reset-wins" b="patterns/latch/alarm-set-wins" scenario="b" caption="Tick 3: `ack` while the fault is still present. The reset-dominant alarm goes off and comes back; the set-dominant one stays on." />

In both charts the alarm stays latched after the fault itself has gone (tick 5): the operator must still
acknowledge it. That is the point of a latch.

In SCCharts, *who wins* is decided by the triggers you write, and by priority when several transitions
of one state are enabled together: the transition written first is taken. So decide the coincidence
explicitly, and put a tick in your test scenario where the two arrive together.

## One writer per variable

So far one region does all the work. It is tempting to split it: one region that switches the lamp on
when the button is pressed, another that switches it off when the lift arrives. KIELER refuses:

<Sim example="patterns/latch/two-writers" caption="KIELER rejects this chart: two concurrent regions write `lamp`." />

Official KIELER reports `Can't schedule from …` (several times) and `The SCG is NOT asc-schedulable!`;
SCCharts Lab adds the readable version:

```text wrap
Circular dependency involving lamp prevents scheduling this tick.
```

Two concurrent regions may not write different values to the same variable, and KIELER checks that
without looking at the triggers: it does not matter that `button` and `arrived` might never be true
together. ([Why the compiler says no](/learn/causality/) explains the rule and its exceptions; writing
the *same* value from several regions, `lamp = true` and `lamp = true`, is allowed.)

The cure is a principle worth adopting everywhere: **every variable has exactly one owner**, the one
region that writes it. Other regions do not write the variable; they send requests to its owner, and a
signal is the natural way to send one:

<Sim example="patterns/latch/one-owner" caption="Two buttons, one owner: `Hall` and `Car` emit `request`, and only `Latch` writes `lamp`." />

`Hall` and `Car` each emit `request`, and several regions may emit the same signal in one tick (tick
7): a signal is present if anyone emits it. `Latch` reads `request` in the same tick, after every
emission, and owns `lamp` alone. The owner is also the one place where the coincidence rules live, which
is a good reason to keep it that way. A shared device works the same way: the
[arbiter](/patterns/arbiter/) that hands a printer to one computer at a time is the one owner of the
variable that says who has it.

## The latch as an equation

If you have met latches in digital logic, you may prefer to write one as an equation. The lamp is on if
it was on in the previous tick or the button is pressed, unless the lift is here:

<Sim example="patterns/latch/latch-equation" caption="Tick for tick the same as `CallButtonFixed` for these inputs." />

`pre(lamp)` is the lamp's value at the end of the previous tick, so the equation holds the lamp on from
one tick to the next; `&& !arrived` makes it reset-dominant. To make it set-dominant, move the button
outside: `lamp = button || (pre(lamp) && !arrived)`. The two equations differ only in a tick where a
press and the lift come together:

<Compare a="patterns/latch/latch-equation" b="patterns/latch/latch-equation-set" scenario="b" caption="Tick 4: a press while the lift is here. The reset-dominant `LatchEquation` keeps the lamp off; the set-dominant `LatchEquationSet` keeps it on." />

The equation is compact, and it is easy to get the dominance wrong by moving a bracket. The two-state
version is easier to extend, for example with a state for "lamp blinking because the lift is on its
way". There is one more difference: the equation runs in an `immediate during` action, so it already
reacts to a press in tick 1, whereas the delayed transition out of `Idle` does not.

## Try it

<TryIt example="patterns/latch/two-floors-starter" solution="patterns/latch/two-floors" title="Two floors">
The starter registers calls on floor 1. Extend it to floor 2, with its own button, lamp and arrival
input (`button2`, `lamp2`, `at2`). The two calls must be independent: serving floor 1 must not clear the
call on floor 2. Keep one writer per lamp. To test it: a call on floor 1 in tick 2 and on floor 2 in
tick 3; the lift at floor 1 in tick 5 clears `lamp1` only, and at floor 2 in tick 7 clears `lamp2`.
</TryIt>

<TryIt example="patterns/latch/queue-starter" solution="patterns/latch/queue" title="Take a number">
A latch remembers one request. A queue counter remembers how many: customers take tickets (`take`),
the counter calls them (`serve`), and `waiting` says how many are waiting. The starter forgets a
service when `take` and `serve` arrive in the same tick: it counts the new ticket and ignores the
call. Fix it, and make sure that `serve` with nobody waiting changes nothing. To test it: `take` in
ticks 2 and 4 and `serve` in tick 6 leave `waiting` at 1; `take` and `serve` together in tick 8
should keep it at 1 (the starter says 2); `serve` in ticks 10 and 12 should give 0, then still 0.
</TryIt>

## Check your understanding

<Predict
  example="patterns/latch/call-button-predict"
  ask={[{ tick: 4, value: 'lamp' }, { tick: 6, value: 'lamp' }, { tick: 7, value: 'lamp' }]}
  caption="`CallButtonFixed`, from the comparison above. In tick 4 a press comes together with the lift; in tick 6 someone presses while the doors are open, and holds the button as the lift leaves in tick 7. Is the lamp on after ticks 4, 6 and 7?"
/>

<details>
<summary>Explanation</summary>

The lamp is off after ticks 4 and 6 and on after tick 7. In tick 4 the press comes with the lift:
`Waiting` looks only at `arrived`, so the call is served and the exit action switches the lamp off. In
tick 6 the doors are open, and `Idle`'s `if button && !arrived` is false. In tick 7 the lift leaves
while the button is still held: now `button && !arrived` holds, and the call is registered.

</details>

<Quiz
  question="An alarm is latched with an equation. Which one is set-dominant, so that a fault wins over an acknowledgement in the same tick?"
  options={['`alarm = (pre(alarm) || fault) && !ack`', '`alarm = pre(alarm) || fault || !ack`', '`alarm = fault || (pre(alarm) && !ack)`']}
  answer={2}
>
With `fault` outside the bracket, a fault switches the alarm on whatever `ack` says, and `ack` can only
clear an alarm that is held from the previous tick. The first equation is reset-dominant: `&& !ack`
switches the alarm off even while the fault lasts. The third is no latch at all: with `|| !ack` the
alarm is on in every tick without an acknowledgement.
</Quiz>

<Quiz
  question="Two regions both contain a transition with the effect `alarm = true`, triggered by different sensors. A third region resets the alarm with `alarm = false`. What does KIELER say?"
  options={['It rejects the chart: concurrent regions write different values', 'It accepts the chart: the writes happen in different ticks', 'It accepts the chart: `true` and `false` are constants']}
  answer={0}
>
The two `alarm = true` writes agree and would be accepted on their own. But `alarm = false` in a
concurrent region writes a different value, and KIELER rejects that, whatever the triggers say. Give
`alarm` one owner and let the other regions emit signals to it.
</Quiz>

<KeyIdeas>
- A latch remembers a request until it is served. A state is the simplest memory; entry and exit
  actions keep an output in step with it.
- Decide what happens when the request and the service arrive in the same tick (reset-dominant or
  set-dominant), write the decision into the triggers, and test that tick.
- One writer per variable: concurrent regions may not assign different values to the same variable, even
  if they never do it in the same tick. Send requests to the owner with signals.
- `x = (pre(x) || set) && !reset` is a reset-dominant latch in one line; `x = set || (pre(x) && !reset)`
  is set-dominant.
</KeyIdeas>
